# QuestKeeper Roadmap

> **Your character. Your party. Every legendary session.**

QuestKeeper is growing from a campaign archive into a lasting home for tabletop
stories: a place players enjoy visiting and Game Masters can maintain without
turning campaign night into a second job.

This roadmap describes the experience we want to build over the next twelve
months. It intentionally focuses on what people will be able to do—not on the
technology behind it. Priorities may move as real parties use the project and
share feedback.

**Roadmap window:** October 2026–October 2027  
**Current destination:** QuestKeeper 2.0

---

## Now · A home for the whole party

### October–December 2026

QuestKeeper should feel useful to players, not only to the person preparing the
campaign.

By this checkpoint, parties should be able to:

- recognise every adventurer immediately through beautiful, consistent profiles;
- keep public stories separate from personal or spoiler-protected notes;
- revisit character growth level by level;
- catch up on recent sessions without searching old messages;
- understand friendships, promises and tensions within the party;
- comfortably use the Companion from a phone before or during a session;
- share or print a character sheet that still feels part of the campaign world.

**The promise:** open QuestKeeper five minutes before game night and remember
who you are, what happened and why it matters.

---

## Next · Ready for game night

### January–March 2027

The Companion will become the party's starting point before every session and
its memory shortly afterwards.

Players and Game Masters can expect:

- a focused game-night view with the latest events, unresolved threads and party status;
- short catch-ups alongside complete session chapters;
- memorable moments that connect characters, places, NPCs and discoveries;
- better personal journals for thoughts that belong to a specific character;
- support for both Discord session notes and traditional handwritten notes;
- a review step that keeps the Game Master in control before new material is published.

**The promise:** less time asking “what happened last time?” and more time
making the next choice count.

---

## Spring · A world that remembers

### April–June 2027

Campaign information should feel connected like a living world instead of
being stored in separate pages.

This stage will bring:

- one search across heroes, sessions, NPCs, places and important clues;
- a campaign timeline that shows how the adventure has changed;
- relationships that evolve as the story progresses;
- easier journeys between a session and the people or places mentioned in it;
- clearer records of open mysteries, promises and unfinished business;
- stronger multilingual campaigns, including tools for spotting missing translations.

**The promise:** when someone remembers a name, place or half-forgotten clue,
the rest of its story is never far away.

---

## Summer · Your chronicle, your style

### July–September 2027

Every table has its own tone. QuestKeeper should reflect that without demanding
design or coding experience.

Parties will gain:

- campaign themes for heroic fantasy, gothic horror and other moods;
- clearer creative guidance for portraits, covers, maps and journal artwork;
- reusable campaign and session templates;
- a friendlier manager that guides new Game Masters from an empty folder to a published chronicle;
- safer backup, restoration and campaign transfer tools;
- ways to reuse original, legally shareable structures without bundling copyrighted adventures.

**The promise:** a campaign can look and feel unique while remaining simple to
maintain and safe to move.

---

## Destination · QuestKeeper 2.0

### October 2027

QuestKeeper 2.0 will unite the player Companion, campaign manager and optional
session tools into one polished journey.

The release is ready when:

- a new Game Master can create and publish a campaign without editing data files;
- a player can reach their character, latest recap and party bonds in seconds;
- campaign histories remain readable after months or years of play;
- desktop, mobile, keyboard and screen-reader experiences are dependable;
- importing, backing up and restoring a chronicle feels safe;
- the public demo honestly shows the full experience before anyone installs it;
- the project remains self-hosted, account-free and welcoming to different systems and languages.

---

## Principles that will not change

### The party owns its story

Campaign files remain portable and under the group's control. QuestKeeper does
not require a subscription or player accounts.

### Players deserve more than a data sheet

Character statistics matter, but so do memories, relationships, artwork,
private thoughts and the choices that changed the adventure.

### The Game Master stays in control

Generated drafts, transcripts and imported notes are suggestions until the Game
Master reviews and publishes them.

### Every table is different

QuestKeeper will remain adaptable to different languages, campaign tones and
tabletop systems instead of becoming tied to one published setting.

### Original worlds belong here

The project will not bundle copied rulebooks, commercial adventures or artwork
that cannot be legally redistributed.
